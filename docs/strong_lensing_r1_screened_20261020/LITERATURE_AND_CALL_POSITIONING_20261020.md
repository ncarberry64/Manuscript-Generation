# Literature and call positioning for the BHSM lens-transfer paper

This is an AI-assisted research assessment, based on primary author/publisher
records checked on 20 October 2026. It introduces no new numerical result and
does not certify novelty by an exhaustive search or predict an editorial
decision. The intended paper has one argument:

**A specified envelope state fixes a normalized encounter transfer; the transfer
fixes image directions and observer delay differences; ordinary lens freedoms
determine which state properties those observables identify.**

BHSM organizes that question. The evaluated action and branch are conditional
R1 inputs. The paper need not establish a distinguishable departure from
conventional gravity to give lens modelers a useful result. Its positive
effective-mass representation is part of the answer to the same inference
question, rather than a second independent contribution to be added to the
scalar field.

unauthenticated request. An alternate official presentation was also
unavailable. Consequently this assessment does not invent or quote a complete

There is a more specific verified fit precedent than the issue title alone:
the publisher identifies [Schechter's 2026 methods article](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/14/2/20)
as belonging to this same special issue. It examines an explicitly restricted
analytic forward lens model and its practical virtues and shortcomings. Thus
the inference that a bounded, reproducible forward-model and degeneracy study
fits the issue is supported by an actual issue contribution. That precedent
does not establish acceptance of this manuscript or support a microscopic BHSM
closure claim. No submission target date is included here.

The strongest defensible contribution is therefore the **controlled and
reproducible forward calculation together with its explicit identifiability

is the separation of a fixed-reference delay offset from a response that can
also be represented by ordinary effective mass, with normalization and common
preparation tracked throughout. The absolute offset alone is not the claim.

## Manuscript changes that improve that single argument

The introduction should pose the state/transfer/inference question and then
state the bounded contribution in one paragraph. Place the short retained
action, state/boundary prescription and source normalization before the optical
application. Keep the main image/delay table, common-reference explanation and
effective-mass construction together. Follow them with the finite nuisance
application and a compact accuracy statement. This sequence answers the same
question throughout.

The separate cap reductions, two-lift microscopic nonuniqueness witnesses and
exact time-dependent bulk continuation are useful source and scope records.
They should be supplementary derivations or a short pointer from the main
paper, rather than coequal main narratives. They explain what the selected
state/action does not determine; their derivation does not add another
calculated optical response. The constant-target null likewise serves as a
variable/boundary control, not an alternative central paper.

Two acceptance-strengthening checks are especially relevant to that argument:

- A controlled smooth density/profile comparison with independently fixed
  mass and scale tests dependence on the convenient compact body. Preserve the
  same-profile conventional comparison and do not tune a profile to strengthen
  the result.
- A finite ordinary-lens nuisance re-fit tests whether the local projector's
  conclusion survives a concrete nonlinear application. Report the achieved
  residual and admitted parameter domain; do not replace this with a larger
  list of abstract degeneracies.

These checks have now been executed in the
[matched-profile application](LENS_MODEL_ROBUSTNESS_20261020.md) and
[finite nonlinear re-fit](NONLINEAR_LENS_REFIT_20261020.md), with seven and four
focused tests respectively. The separately executed
[distributed-encounter control](ENCOUNTER_ACCURACY_CONTROL_20261020.md)
adds a same-preparation optical accuracy application, with seven focused

## Smooth-profile reference: Plummer

The original publisher record verifies H. C. Plummer's 1911 profile paper,
[MNRAS 71, 460–470](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/71/5/460/972150),
DOI [10.1093/mnras/71.5.460](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/71.5.460).
It is a suitable citation for the standard regular control. For the stated
density, the following projection follows directly by integration and need
not be claimed as a new result:

\[
\rho_P(r)=\frac{3Ma^2}{4\pi(r^2+a^2)^{5/2}},\quad
M_P(<r)=\frac{Mr^3}{(r^2+a^2)^{3/2}},
\]
\[
\Sigma_P(b)=\frac{Ma^2}{\pi(b^2+a^2)^2},\qquad
M_{P,2D}(<b)=\frac{Mb^2}{b^2+a^2},\qquad
r_{1/2}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2^{2/3}-1}}.
\]

Matching total mass and the three-dimensional half-mass radius fixes the
control's scale without a desired-deflection fit. It tests smoothness/profile
dependence. It is not an empirical galaxy model or an evolved material EOS.
Its infinite support also requires its own declared tail/aperture treatment;
the compact body's material radius must not silently become a Plummer edge.
