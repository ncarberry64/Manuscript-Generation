# Cover letter draft — Galaxies

**Manuscript:** Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation

**Special issue:** Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling

**Author:** Norman P. Carberry

Dear Dr. Schuldt and Editors of *Galaxies*,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation,” for the special issue “Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling.”

The study asks how BHSM surface contributions determine gravitational lensing and time delays for light traversing an envelope event. Its central object is the incoming-to-outgoing light transfer, conditional on the envelope state and surrounding geometry. A previously specified R1 cubic scalar–tensor effective action supplies the evaluated source, metric constraint and regular finite-body response. The physical source position, measured redshifts, observer clock and compatible cosmological distance maps are shared across the calculation.

For the declared example, the regular matter-sourced R1 branch has a screening radius of approximately 212.0292 kpc. Its delay offsets relative to the specified fixed-body reference are approximately 25.95925815 s and 8.64901938 s. Applying the same finite-body second-order general-relativistic encounter correction to both calculations gives 25.95932714 s and 8.64900924 s, including the central light ray. This checks one common conventional correction. The earlier constant-target null result is preserved within its separate boundary problem.

The conventional gravitational calculation supplies both the benchmark and a familiar representation of the same retained response. A positive effective-mass construction reproduces the complete leading scalar gravitational and optical field. We present that equivalence as a constructive result in the BHSM program; a distinguishable departure from conventional gravity is not the publication objective. The single-source and multi-source nuisance results state which response combinations the specified observations constrain. The equivalent effective mass and scalar response are counted once.

We also evaluate the coupled effective source, its nonlinear scalar flux and
material junction, and construct two positive same-domain lifts showing which
microscopic source-map and complement data the leading response does not select.
Exact higher-order scalar/metric, material, environmental and matching equations
are now derived and algebraically checked. An evolved physical continuation
requires the physical surface-to-R1 embedding, a material EOS/support relation,
and environmental/outer Cauchy and matching data. These derived equations are
distinguished from a completed evolved solution.

The contribution is relevant to the special issue through its treatment of time-delay calculations, mass-sheet and profile degeneracies, common optical preparation, and reproducible numerical methods. The source package contains the derivations, code, numerical outputs, independent computational checks and commands needed to repeat the calculation. Substantive AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript, with scientific responsibility remaining with the human author.
