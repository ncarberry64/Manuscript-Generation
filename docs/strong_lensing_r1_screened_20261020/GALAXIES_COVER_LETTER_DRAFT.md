# Cover letter draft — Galaxies

**Manuscript:** Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Calculation and an Observed-Light Test

**Special issue:** Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling

**Author:** Norman P. Carberry

Dear Dr. Schuldt and Editors of *Galaxies*,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Calculation and an Observed-Light Test,” for the special issue “Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling.”

The paper asks how a specified envelope state determines incoming-to-outgoing light transfer and what lensing observations can identify about that response. In the BHSM interpretation, environmental light probes an envelope event conditional on its state and surrounding geometry. A declared R1 scalar–tensor effective action supplies a normalized local source and regular finite-body response. Conventional gravity supplies the benchmark and a positive effective-mass representation of the same retained response, counted once. The argument follows source, metric/optical transfer, images and delays, then empirical identification under ordinary lens freedoms.

We now execute that baseline-to-prediction sequence with public COSMOGRAIL monitoring of the well-observed four-image lens HE 0435-1223 and published HST point astrometry. The endpoint of an earlier independent monitoring release fixes a chronological boundary: 902 epochs from a common later reduction train the models, and 100 later epochs are withheld. Predictions and source/data hashes were committed before target scoring. The primary test conditions explicitly on the later B light curve and predicts later A/C/D; it is retrospective batch cross-image prediction. A secondary test forecasts all later images using historical training alone. No reserved target values determine fitted parameters or model selection.

The observed pilot reports a limitation rather than a detection. The R1-minus-conventional primary joint conditional log-score difference is only +0.00498, providing no persuasive separation. Both macro models achieve 85.33% coverage for nominal 95% marginal intervals, falling to 75.50% in the future-only test; image-level results expose the shared variability model's calibration problem. The complete source-derived effective-mass representation reproduces the retained R1 transfer by structural equality, not by an independent empirical confirmation. A forward image search additionally predicts a central fifth image at about 6.3% of A's macro flux, without an imposed observational non-detection limit. These inadequacies remain visible and the consumed test is not retuned into a new result.

Synthetic finite conventional re-fits, matched compact/smooth profiles, original scalar/metric residuals, common finite-body GR corrections and distributed-encounter controls support the same transfer-to-identification argument. The study uses established screening and lensing methods without claiming their discovery. Its observed contribution is an explicit, reproducible conditional comparison and a documented failure of adequate predictive calibration; it does not identify a unique microscopic BHSM state, solve the global environment or validate the adopted background cosmology. The local prescribed-density source, remote optical screen, covariance approximation and point-parameter predictive uncertainty are stated. Physical admission remains false with the original calibration and 2e-10 guard unchanged.

The source package contains original public-data provenance and attribution, derivations, frozen training and prediction records, one target application, focused tests and separate source/likelihood reviews. A labeled known-outcome replay reproduces the archived result and is not a second independent test. Substantive AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript, with scientific responsibility remaining with the human author. These features connect the paper to lens modeling, time-delay prediction, degeneracy and model adequacy without making an acceptance or observational discovery claim.

I am the sole author and accept responsibility for the scientific content. This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration at another journal. The research received no external funding, and I declare no conflicts of interest. The public research code and working manuscript are disclosed in the data-availability statement.
