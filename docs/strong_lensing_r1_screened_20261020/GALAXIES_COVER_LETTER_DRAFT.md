# Cover letter draft — Galaxies

**Manuscript:** Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation

**Special issue:** Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling

**Author:** Norman P. Carberry

Dear Dr. Schuldt and Editors of *Galaxies*,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger–Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation,” for the special issue “Strong Gravitational Lens Modeling.”

The study asks how BHSM surface contributions determine gravitational lensing and time delays for light traversing an envelope event. Its central object is the incoming-to-outgoing light transfer, conditional on the envelope state and surrounding geometry. A previously specified R1 cubic scalar–tensor effective action supplies the evaluated source, metric constraint and regular finite-body response. The physical source position, measured redshifts, observer clock and compatible cosmological distance maps are shared across the calculation.

For the declared example, the regular matter-sourced R1 branch has a screening radius of approximately 212.0292 kpc. Its delay offsets relative to the specified fixed-body reference are approximately 25.95925815 s and 8.64901938 s. Applying the same finite-body second-order general-relativistic encounter correction to both calculations gives 25.95932714 s and 8.64900924 s, including the central light ray. This checks one common conventional correction. The earlier constant-target null result is preserved within its separate boundary problem.

The conventional gravitational calculation supplies both the benchmark and a familiar representation of the same retained response. A positive effective-mass construction reproduces the complete leading scalar gravitational and optical field. We then execute a finite nonlinear conventional re-fit of the three axial images and two relative delays. Allowing mass, scale, source position, lens center and one physical mass sheet absorbs this example, with independently re-solved image roots and delay residuals below a nanosecond. This is a deliberately saturated local inverse example, not an observational likelihood or a general uniqueness result. The equivalent effective mass and scalar response are counted once.

Matched compact Tolman–VII and smooth Plummer profiles, with the same total mass
and three-dimensional half-mass radius, show how the forward result depends on
ordinary profile freedom. Their conventional delays differ by more than a day,
while the corresponding reference-relative scalar offsets change by a few
seconds. State-coefficient derivatives expose which combinations of the
conditional effective response the light probes; a physical BHSM state
variation still requires the state-to-coefficient map. These controls and the
common finite-body GR correction support the same transfer-to-inference
argument. They do not supply an astrophysical uncertainty distribution.

A distributed-encounter calculation then applies the same retained weak-field
paraxial action between exterior wavefronts fixed separately by the closed-FLRW
background. At a common 20 Mpc half-window, it changes the two state-dependent
delay offsets by approximately −0.619 ms and +0.0010 ms. This supplies a concrete
finite-width/path control and an explicitly scoped isolated-field tail bound;
it does not claim a completed higher-order scalar metric or global match.

Supplementary records evaluate the coupled effective source, nonlinear flux
and material junction, and give explicit positive-lift witnesses of the
microscopic source-map nonuniqueness. Exact higher-order scalar/metric,
material, environmental and matching equations are derived and checked. An
evolved physical continuation still requires the physical surface-to-R1
embedding, material EOS/support and environmental/outer Cauchy and matching
data. The focused paper distinguishes these derived relations from a selected
microscopic state or an evolved physical solution.

The contribution is a reproducible conditional forward model together with an
explicit identifiability limit, relevant to the special issue through
time-delay modeling, finite mass-sheet/profile re-fits and common optical
preparation. Its practical endpoint is a prospective workflow in which
archival observations calibrate a conditional envelope optical map, frozen
predictions face held-out data or new monitoring, and subsequent observations
refine that map. The present study is synthetic and does not establish unique
microscopic envelope states. We use established cubic-screening and lensing methods without
claiming their discovery. The source package contains derivations, code,
numerical outputs and independent computational checks. Substantive AI
assistance is disclosed in the manuscript, with scientific responsibility
remaining with the human author.

I am the sole author and accept responsibility for the scientific content.
This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration at
another journal. The research received no external funding, and I declare
