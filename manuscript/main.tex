\begin{abstract}
We present a geometry-first alternative to representation-first effective
modeling and develop its cosmological consequences within the Berger--Hopf
Standard Model (BHSM) program.  The central methodological distinction is
that physical degrees of freedom are not assumed at the outset as fundamental
fields.  They are identified only after variation of the underlying action,
enforcement of constraints and boundary conditions, quotienting of gauge
redundancies, and reduction of the physical Hessian.  Field-like variables
then arise as coordinates on the surviving physical response rather than as
prior ontological assumptions.

We apply this workflow to a closed hyperspherical cosmological parent
$S^3(R_H)$.  The exceptional lowest scalar harmonic is separated from the
first ordinary physical scalar carrier, yielding a reduced physical
$n=2$ phase-space state $X_2=(q_2,\Pi_2)^T$.  Metric response follows from the
same constraint-reduced Hessian through a Schur/Feshbach map rather than from
an independently postulated scalar--metric coupling.  The resulting state is
propagated to observables using a gauge-invariant closed-$S^3$
luminosity-distance kernel and a Jacobi/Sachs optical transport system for
photon redshift, convergence, and shear.

Local unresolved geometry is treated as stochastic topographic response rather
than as an exhaustive sum over foreground objects.  This leads to a finite
optical state and covariance evolution and, for $m$ physical topographic
texture channels, the normalization-independent prediction
$\operatorname{rank}C_{\rm topo}\le m$ for the isolated cross-observable
covariance.  In the one-channel limit the topographic covariance is rank one
and all $2\times2$ covariance minors vanish.

Intermediate effective-field representations are retained as compatibility
languages rather than as foundational definitions of the theory.  A
curvature-dependent constraint-preservation defect encountered in one such
representation illustrates why agreement at the level of effective equations
does not by itself establish equivalence to the underlying constraint-reduced
geometry.  The framework is deliberately falsifiable: homogeneous
state-selection models and multiple preregistered foreground proxies fail
held-out tests and are rejected rather than retuned.  The surviving
construction yields prospective tests involving luminosity distance, growth,
lensing, redshift perturbations, common angular structure, and
cross-observable covariance.
\end{abstract}
