\begin{abstract}
We develop a late-time cosmological bridge between a scalar topographic effective field theory on compact spatial sections \(S^3(R_H)\) and the constraint-reduced response architecture of the Berger--Hopf program. A closed-FLRW gauge audit changes the identification of the relevant infrared carrier: the first nonconstant scalar harmonic in our convention, \(n=1\), maps to the exceptional \(N=2\) sector of standard closed-universe perturbation theory and is nondynamical or pure gauge in the regular single-field covariant scalar sector. The first propagating scalar harmonic is therefore our \(n=2\) (\(N=3\) in the alternative convention). This does not remove the sky-dipole mechanism. A general degree-two scalar harmonic on \(S^3\) contains observer-sky monopole, dipole, and quadrupole pieces, and a pure cross-term subspace produces an exact sky dipole proportional to \(\sin[2\psi(z)]\,\hat{\mathbf n}\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf p}\). The reduced Schur/Feshbach response can selectively soften this first physical harmonic while stiffening all propagating \(n\ge3\) modes when \(1/15<\ell^2<1/8\). Reference Branch R1 and its frozen numerical manifest are left unchanged because the background and high-\(n\) growth calculation do not depend on the identity of the curvature-scale carrier. The theoretical harmonic identification is amended prospectively before comparison with the targeted independent data. The resulting framework is falsifiable through curvature, the fixed dipole axis and radial envelope, compact-harmonic hierarchy, growth, and lensing tests.
\end{abstract}
