\Title{Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger--Hopf Framework: An R1 Calculation and an Observed-Light Test}
\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0000-6650-3485}
\Author{Norman P. Carberry \orcidA{}}
\AuthorNames{Norman P. Carberry}
\address[1]{Independent Researcher, Oconomowoc, Wisconsin, USA;\\
Correspondence: \href{mailto:carberry8878130@hotmail.com}{carberry8878130@hotmail.com}}
\abstract{Prepared environmental light probes state-dependent optical transfer in the Berger--Hopf interpretation. We derive a conditional strong-lensing response from the specified R1 cubic scalar--tensor action. A regular finite body produces delay offsets of 25.9593 s and 8.6490 s; a positive conventional effective mass reproduces the retained metric. A first HE 0435-1223 test undercovers: nominal 95\% intervals contain 85.33\% of reserved magnitudes. Actual HST F814W pixels disfavor its unattenuated central/A prediction of 0.0632 within the tested PSF/noise family; the conservative numerical envelope is below 0.030. A compact-source revision reduces that ratio to 0.00264 without measuring a nuclear mass. Revised predictions are frozen before opening unused LCO catalogue targets. With image C as driver, both macro models cover 160 of 162 A/B/D targets, with RMSE 0.0772 mag and mean 95\% interval width 0.670 mag. The intervals are conservative rather than sharply calibrated. A separately frozen Gaia test gives 3.26 mas coordinate RMS and fails the catalogue-error-only check for both models. These source and predictive constraints establish neither a uniquely identified envelope state nor a BHSM detection.}
\keyword{Berger--Hopf framework; strong gravitational lensing; time delays; envelope response; Vainshtein screening; effective-mass representation}
