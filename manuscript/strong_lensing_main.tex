\Title{Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger--Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation}
\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0000-6650-3485}
\Author{Norman P. Carberry \orcidA{}}
\AuthorNames{Norman P. Carberry}
\address[1]{Independent Researcher, Oconomowoc, Wisconsin, USA;\\
Correspondence: \href{mailto:carberry8878130@hotmail.com}{carberry8878130@hotmail.com}}
\abstract{In the Berger--Hopf interpretation, the optical transfer of prepared environmental light depends on the envelope state. We construct a reproducible strong-lensing benchmark from a specified R1 cubic scalar--tensor action and ask which properties of that transfer ordinary lens models can identify. A common incident marker, physical source, observer clock, redshifts and distance maps define the comparison. The regular source determines the scalar and metric response without a fitted local amplitude. For the declared \texorpdfstring{$10^{11}M_\odot$}{100 billion solar masses}, 3 kpc body, its screening radius is 212.0292 kpc and its fixed-body-reference delay offsets are 25.9593 s and 8.6490 s. A positive conventional effective mass reproduces the complete retained response. A finite five-parameter conventional refit also reproduces the three injected images and two delays to numerical precision. A smooth profile matched by mass and half-mass radius changes the offsets while preserving this equivalence. State-coefficient derivatives and common-gravity and distributed-encounter controls quantify the transfer's dependencies and approximation hierarchy. The result is a conditional forward and inverse benchmark, with a microscopic BHSM source embedding and evolved outer state identified separately.}
