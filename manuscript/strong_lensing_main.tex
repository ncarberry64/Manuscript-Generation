\begin{abstract}
We compare a conventional galaxy lens with a conditional Berger--Hopf
envelope encounter using the same incoming light and physical source
screen. Three measured-redshift Jacobi maps in a spatially modulated closed
cosmology determine image positions and relative arrival times. For one
normalized axial harmonic, six conditional state columns have rank three
with nuisances fixed. An analytic factorization explains their rank-zero
projection when source, galaxy and mass-sheet freedoms are unrestricted.
The isothermal control retains a $17.094532$-day relative delay. We also
construct a regular, source-free family of constant $S^6$ target states
around a finite Tolman--VII fluid body. The target equations and currents
vanish exactly, while the normalized phase stiffness is positive. An
explicit common-domain metric/phase Hessian on the Schwarzschild exterior
has an invertible metric block and zero mixed response. The solved body
produces a nonzero optical encounter, equal to the conventional encounter
for the same matter profile; intrinsic mass and radius derivatives are
included in image and delay responses. This equivalence is conditional on
the retained four-dimensional gravity sector and specified source-free
boundary data. A nonzero interface response and the general matching of an
exported topographic scalar to the target phase remain open constitutive
questions.
\end{abstract}
