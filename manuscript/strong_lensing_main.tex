\Title{Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger--Hopf Framework: An R1 Effective Calculation}
\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0000-6650-3485}
\Author{Norman P. Carberry \orcidA{}}
\AuthorNames{Norman P. Carberry}
\address[1]{Independent Researcher, Oconomowoc, Wisconsin, USA;\\
Correspondence: \href{mailto:carberry8878130@hotmail.com}{carberry8878130@hotmail.com}}
\abstract{In the Berger--Hopf framework, gravitational lensing is the incoming-to-outgoing transfer of environmental light through an envelope event. We investigate how its surface contributions can be represented in a conditional R1 effective calculation, keeping the microscopic common-source derivation distinct from the response already computed. A fixed closed background and cubic scalar--tensor action determine a regular finite-body metric response without a fitted local amplitude. Common incident light, measured redshifts, source coordinates and observer clocks define image directions and relative arrival times. For a \texorpdfstring{$10^{11}M_\odot$}{100 billion solar masses}, 3 kpc body at redshift 0.3 and a source at redshift 1, the screening radius is 212.0292 kpc. Relative to the specified fixed-body reference, the leading delay offsets are 25.9593 s and 8.6490 s. A positive conventional effective-mass profile reproduces the complete retained metric and optical response: the matched representations agree. A common finite-body general-relativistic second-order control changes the reference-relative offsets by less than 0.1 ms. Nuisance calculations identify interchangeable source and profile contributions. Explicit source applications and continuation equations advance the BHSM transfer calculation. Positive complement constructions show why microscopic matching needs additional physical data; material and outer-state prescriptions are required for further evolution.}
