\begin{abstract}
We construct a reproducible comparison between a conventional galaxy lens
and the optical response available from the Berger--Hopf envelope equations,
using a common incident-light preparation and physical source-screen
convention. The frozen R1 closed cosmology supplies propagation; a separate
galaxy encounter supplies the illustrative strong deflection. Three
compatible Jacobi maps determine the lens equation and arrival-time
functional. We retain source-coordinate, impact-parameter and
measured-redshift endpoint changes in the response. The coupled cosmological
provider evolves six physical anchor amplitudes for one selected normalized
spatial harmonic. The established static spatial phase channel has zero
mixed metric response when its spatial current vanishes; this restricted
null does not establish absence of other geometric responses. Conditional
responses are separated from action-normalized predictions and tested
against independently re-solved images. Nuisance projection explicitly
retains source, lens, mass-sheet, line-of-sight and cosmological freedoms.
The illustrative isothermal benchmark has images at $+1.067883$ and
$-0.711922$ arcsec and a relative delay of $17.094532$ days. The six
conditional state responses have rank three with nuisances fixed and rank
zero after the declared unrestricted nuisance projection. A small
specified scalar-state probe changes the delay by $-0.01718$ seconds; its
amplitude is not an action-derived envelope calibration. The checkpoint
identifies the local common-domain action projections needed for a
galaxy-replacement prediction.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} strong gravitational lensing; time delays;
closed cosmology; Jacobi maps; mass-sheet degeneracy; Berger--Hopf framework
