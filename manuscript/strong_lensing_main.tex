\Title{Strong-Lensing Time Delays in the Berger--Hopf Framework: An R1 Calculation and an Observed-Light Test}
\newcommand{\orcidauthorA}{0009-0000-6650-3485}
\Author{Norman P. Carberry \orcidA{}}
\AuthorNames{Norman P. Carberry}
\address[1]{Independent Researcher, Oconomowoc, Wisconsin, USA;\\
Correspondence: \href{mailto:carberry8878130@hotmail.com}{carberry8878130@hotmail.com}}
\abstract{Prepared environmental light probes an envelope's state-dependent optical transfer in the Berger--Hopf interpretation. We construct a strong-lensing benchmark from the specified R1 cubic scalar--tensor action and test what ordinary lens freedoms allow its observables to identify. A common source, incident marker, observer clock and distance maps fix the synthetic comparison. A regular finite-body source determines the response without a fitted local amplitude. The declared \texorpdfstring{$10^{11}M_\odot$}{100 billion solar masses}, 3 kpc body has a screening radius of 212.0292 kpc and fixed-body-reference delay offsets of 25.9593 s and 8.6490 s. A positive conventional effective mass reproduces the retained response; a finite conventional refit also matches the injected images and delays. Profile, coefficient and distributed-ray controls quantify the dependencies. We then fit a restricted HE 0435-1223 baseline to 902 historical epochs and reserve 100 later epochs. With later image B as an allowed driver, conventional and conditional R1 predictions for 300 withheld magnitudes differ by only 0.00498 in joint log score. Their nominal 95\% intervals cover 85.33\% of targets, exposing shared brightness-model limitations. The resulting conditional transfer benchmark and retrospective test establish neither a unique microscopic envelope state nor a BHSM detection.}
\keyword{Berger--Hopf framework; strong gravitational lensing; time delays; envelope response; Vainshtein screening; effective-mass representation}
