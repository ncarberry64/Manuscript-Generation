
\section{Discussion}

The bridge developed here differs from several common late-time alternatives in a specific way. A local-flow explanation can generate a low-redshift dipole but does not by itself predict a compact \(S^3\) harmonic hierarchy, a frozen curvature sign, and a linked high-\(n\) response. Ordinary quintessence has a canonical smooth gradient sector and does not naturally single out one discrete global spatial harmonic. A pure cubic Galileon supplies kinetic braiding but, without an additional scale-selective completion, a negative bare gradient coefficient would not isolate only the deep infrared. The reduced-response term introduced here instead permits a positive selective spatial kernel in which the first nonconstant \(S^3\) level is enhanced relative to higher levels.

The compact spectrum is central. With \(R_H\simeq33\) Gpc, ordinary RSD wavenumbers correspond to harmonics hundreds or thousands of levels above \(n=1\). This makes it possible, at the level of the reduced susceptibility, for a large global low-mode response to coexist with nearly standard galaxy clustering. The model is therefore not equivalent to a scale-independent fifth force.

The same mechanism also clarifies the role of BHSM. The manuscript does not require that a cosmological scalar be identified directly with one microscopic Berger--Hopf field. What is imported is the reduced-response logic: a selected physical branch is evaluated only after the coupled complement has adjusted. In a derivative expansion, the illustrative block model produces a negative long-wave Schur correction together with a positive higher-spatial-gradient stiffness. The specific coupling ansatz \(C(p)=g\sqrt p\), however, is an exploratory bridge realization rather than a microscopic BHSM derivation.

The most important unresolved theoretical issue is the physical metric realization of the first nonconstant scalar harmonic. The scalar pattern on \(S^3\) is exact, but its corresponding closed-FLRW mode is exceptional in standard gauge-invariant perturbation theory \citep{KieferVardanyan2022}. The present work therefore does not claim that the observed distance dipole has already been derived from the metric field equations. The decisive next calculation is a constrained scalar--metric reduction for this exceptional sector, including its physical domain, kinetic matrix, gradient matrix, and light-cone source terms.

Several additional limitations remain. The source law \(S_1\) and the light-cone projection kernel must be derived before the carried-forward Pantheon normalization can be inverted into a unique microscopic susceptibility. \(D_{\rm kin}>0\) is an inherited cubic-Galileon kinetic diagnostic, not a complete closed-background stability proof. The minimal no-slip closure used for exploratory lensing definitions is not assumed to be a final theorem. The R1 values \(q=3\) and \(\lambda=1\) are reference-branch choices, not yet action-derived constants. The frozen curvature forecast is prospective and should not be retuned to current isotropic inference.

These limitations are scientifically useful because each corresponds to a concrete calculation or observation rather than unrestricted fitting freedom.
