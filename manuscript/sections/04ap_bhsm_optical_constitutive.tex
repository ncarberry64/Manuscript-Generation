\section{Constitutive reduction of the BHSM optical source}
\label{sec:bhsm-optical-constitutive}

The optical transfer equation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:bhsm-optical-transfer} isolates two constitutive maps that were
left open: the seam-to-topography export $\mathcal E_\Sigma$ and the local
metric-response map $\mathcal R_{g\psi}$.  The retained BHSM calculations
already reduce both maps further than a generic phenomenological
parameterization.

\subsection{Static and moving seam export}

The retained scalar-topographic flux obeys
\begin{equation}
\mathbf F_T=\nabla T-B\nabla(\nabla^2T),
\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf F_T=S,
\end{equation}
and the canonical-flux acceleration candidate is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf g_T=-Q\mathbf F_T.
\end{equation}
For the stationary spherical neighborhood,
\begin{equation}
Q S_{\rm enc}(r)=\frac{Q_\Sigma}{r^2},
\qquad
Q_\Sigma=V_\Sigma^2,
\qquad
r\ge r_\star.
\end{equation}
Thus the static export profile is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\Sigma^{(0)}[Q_\Sigma]
=
\epsilon_\Sigma
\frac{Q_\Sigma}{Q}
\frac{\Theta(r-r_\star)}{r^2}.
}
\label{eq:static-seam-export}
\end{equation}
The $r^{-2}$ shape is inherited from the conserved-throughput neighborhood
law; $\epsilon_\Sigma$ records the still-open common-action orientation.

If de-encapsulation is represented by the time change of this exported
source, then for a moving spherical seam
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\Sigma^{(1)}
=
\frac{\epsilon_\Sigma}{Q}
\left[
\dot Q_\Sigma\frac{\Theta(r-r_\star)}{r^2}
-
Q_\Sigma\dot r_\star
\frac{\delta(r-r_\star)}{r_\star^2}
\right].
}
\label{eq:dynamic-seam-export}
\end{equation}
The second term is a seam-localized contribution generated by boundary
motion.  Equation~\eqref{eq:dynamic-seam-export} is a minimal kinematic
continuation, not yet a proof that the retained action selects the time
derivative rather than the static source itself.

\subsection{Explicit local metric response}

Here the conventional spatial-potential sign gives Weyl response $(\Phi+\Psi)/2$. The growth section instead uses the positive spatial-curvature perturbation, giving $\Psi-\Phi$; convert the spatial-potential sign before composing maps. Choose a two-coordinate physical scalar metric basis
$h_s=(\Phi,\Psi)^T$ and write
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_g^{\rm loc}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
b&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal B_{g\psi}^{\rm loc}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u\\v
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For $\Delta_g=ac-b^2\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{g\psi}^{\rm loc}
=
-
(\mathcal A_g^{\rm loc})^{-1}
\mathcal B_{g\psi}^{\rm loc}
=
\frac1{\Delta_g}
\begin{pmatrix}
bv-cu\\
bu-av
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\label{eq:local-metric-response-explicit}
\end{equation}
Hence the Weyl/lensing response per unit topographic field is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal R_W
=
\frac{u(b-c)+v(b-a)}
{2(ac-b^2)}.
}
\label{eq:local-weyl-response}
\end{equation}
The local optical metric response therefore reduces to five projected action
matrix elements rather than an arbitrary transfer function.

\subsection{Upstream ownership of optical covariance}

Let
\begin{equation}
C_J
=
C_{\rm grav}
+
\mathcal E_\Sigma C_\Sigma\mathcal E_\Sigma^\dagger
+
C_\xi.
\end{equation}
This sum assumes mutually uncorrelated source sectors. If they are
correlated, their cross-covariance terms must be retained in $C_J$ before
propagation; independence is not implied by the sector names.
With $\mathcal G_\psi=\mathcal L_\psi^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
C_\psi
=
\mathcal G_\psi C_J\mathcal G_\psi^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The unresolved optical forcing covariance is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
Q_{\rm opt}
=
\mathcal O_{\rm opt}
\mathcal R_{g\psi}
\mathcal G_\psi
C_J
\mathcal G_\psi^\dagger
\mathcal R_{g\psi}^\dagger
\mathcal O_{\rm opt}^\dagger.
}
\label{eq:qopt-pullback}
\end{equation}
Thus $Q_{\rm opt}$ is not an independent phenomenological covariance matrix.
Its rank and cross-observable correlations are inherited from the upstream
BHSM source statistics.

For a compound-Poisson rare-event population with event rate
$\lambda_\Sigma$ and seam-source amplitude $q_\Sigma$,
\begin{equation}
C_\Sigma^{\rm shot}
=
\lambda_\Sigma
\langle q_\Sigma q_\Sigma^\dagger\rangle,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm opt}^{\rm shot}
=
\lambda_\Sigma
\mathcal M_\Sigma
\langle q_\Sigma q_\Sigma^\dagger\rangle
\mathcal M_\Sigma^\dagger,
\qquad
\mathcal M_\Sigma
=
\mathcal O_{\rm opt}
\mathcal R_{g\psi}
\mathcal G_\psi
\mathcal E_\Sigma.
\end{equation}

The remaining numerical closure is finite: evaluate the projected local
metric Hessian entries $(a,b,c)$, the mixed entries $(u,v)$, the
common-action seam-export normalization/orientation, and the low-order
statistics of the source population.  These quantities replace the previous
object-by-object foreground reconstruction problem.
