\section{Frozen supernova sightline interpretation test}
\label{sec:sn-sightlines}
We test whether source structure and independently constrained foregrounds
leave a residual following the existing local optical template. This
retrospective interpretation test does not instantiate the prospective
multi-observable gates. The axis $(211.48^\circ,-12.81^\circ)$, amplitude
$A=-0.04321623436$ mag, $z_{\rm ref}=0.02$, $z_c=0.03$, local redshift
transfer, R1 background and original sample cuts remain fixed.
No residual-fitted bulk velocity or external velocity normalization is used.

\subsection{Source and independent foreground construction}
Individual epoch/band photometry for all 3,521 released Pantheon+ and DES
Hubble-diagram rows is locally reconstructed with the released SALT models.
There are 3,480 converged solutions and 41 non-converged local fits, all
retained without changing the Hubble-diagram selection. Flux normalization,
color, shape and peak epoch are separated; remaining chromatic and phase
contrasts are orthogonalized. Band deletion, individual-MJD epoch deletion,
phase-block deletion and released spectral/calibration variants provide
stability diagnostics. These are local-linear responses, not full nonlinear
SNANA/BBC reruns. DES nominal passband proxies and incomplete systematic
mappings remain explicitly flagged.

The common distance residual used with the released total covariance is
the published standardized distance modulus minus the frozen R1 distance.
The raw-fit normalization departure is separate: substituting it into
the released distance vector would require a new propagated covariance.
Reported epoch variances do not supply the complete model, shared
calibration and bias-correction covariance.

Independent density and velocity information comes from the galaxy-only
2MRS neural reconstruction, trained on simulation mocks
\citep{LilowEtAl2024Fields}. Its valid radius is
$200\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$; distant foregrounds remain unmeasured.
Observed 2MRS counts \citep{Huchra2012} are archived separately from the
reconstructed matter-density proxy, with magnitude/sky-selection limits.
Released dust/host measurements, marginal field errors and a local
Green-kernel lensing proxy accompany each sightline. The external spatial
error covariance is unavailable.

\subsection{Conditional held-out likelihoods}
Source/environment nuisance coefficients are learned only on training rows;
held-out rows profile the original redshift-bin monopoles and slopes.
Every observation of a held-out SN is excluded from fold training.
For training-to-test operator $T$, the prediction-error covariance is
\begin{equation}
 V=C_{vv}+T C_{tt}T^{\mathsf T}
       -C_{vt}T^{\mathsf T}-T C_{tv}.
\end{equation}
Pantheon uses its released total covariance. The full DES precision is
inverted before selecting rows. No difference of released covariances is
treated as an independent PV covariance. Unreleased cross-survey systematic
covariance is set to zero only for the explicitly conditional DES transfer;
no combined discovery significance follows.

The primary held-out Pantheon preference is weak and does not transfer to
DES (Table~\ref{tab:sn-sightline-tests}). Matched same-redshift/different-sky,
same-sky/different-redshift and shared-foreground pairs retain their full
contrast covariance. The all-Pantheon preference is concentrated in
high-density sightlines and varies by survey and sky sector. All-Pantheon
cross-fitting reuses the original amplitude-training population, so it is
descriptive rather than independent confirmation. Survey/redshift/sector
permutations preserve duplicate blocks and the exact redshift law, but
their nominal 0.05 tail occurs in 0.09 of the Pantheon null calibration
mocks; permutation tails are therefore auxiliary diagnostics.

\subsection{PV projection, rank and foreground localization}
The saved original low-redshift estimator projects the released PV
correction onto $+0.033953716$ mag, compared with $+0.009368488$ mag for
the independent galaxy-only correction. This demonstrates overlap and
correction sensitivity, not identification of the removed component as
BHSM optical history. The predictive amplitude remains unchanged.
An explicitly \emph{fitted-only} one-scalar diagnostic gives
$A_{\rm fit}=-0.032870\pm0.012398$ mag with direction and redshift dependence
fixed. The source/environment nuisance space retains 79.0\% of the
bin-profiled template information, inflating its uncertainty by 1.125.
No fitted amplitude is used to improve the predictive scores.

An experimental path-length matrix $W_{ik}$ records shared
$10\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ Cartesian cells within the unchanged radial shells,
using $2\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$ midpoint steps. The local matrix has 3,931
sampled columns, with numerical ranks 1,268 and 238 for the Pantheon and
DES row sets at relative singular tolerance $10^{-7}$.
Cell-sharing covariance kernels are transformed through the same residual
operator as the data. Aggregate shell-adjusted mock tails are no
smaller than 0.328 (Pantheon) and 0.531 (DES). A 0.0195 tail in one of 22
overlapping leave-subset scans is not corrected across that family.
These tests establish neither foreground-cell localization nor a coherent
cross-survey frozen residual. They are not a measurement of the isolated
multi-observable topographic covariance or a test of its rank-one gate.

The complete report, compact sightline/results tables, acquisition hashes
and script snapshot are retained under
\texttt{analyses/sn\_sightline\_tomography\_v1} in the repository.
The full data-bearing local archive is identified there by SHA-256;
a public permanent archive remains open. Complete source-reduction and
external-field error propagation are required to resolve the primary
physical interpretation.
