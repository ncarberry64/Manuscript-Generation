\section{BHSM-native transport of the physical topographic mode}
\label{sec:bhsm-native-transport}

The purpose of this section is to separate the ontology of the
Berger--Hopf Standard Model (BHSM) mode from any particular four-dimensional
effective-field-theory coordinate used to represent its late-time response.
The distinction is important in the closed cosmological setting: the physical
topographic carrier is not introduced as an additional free bulk scalar.
Rather, it is an action-owned geometric/boundary mode whose physical dynamics
are obtained only after the BHSM constraints and gauge directions have been
removed.

\subsection{Action ownership and physical tangent space}

The BHSM construction used in this work treats modes as physical
boundary-local fields acted on by sector projectors.  The scalar/topographic
sector is represented in the unified action candidate and admits an
action-reduced mode functional together with a normalized profile-boundary
boundary-value problem.  These ingredients motivate the following physical
tangent space at a background configuration $z_\star$:
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm phys}(z_\star)
=
\ker D\mathcal C(z_\star)\cap\mathcal G^\perp ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal C$ denotes the action-owned constraints and $\mathcal G$
the infinitesimal gauge directions.  The corresponding projected Hessian is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal H}
=
P_{\rm phys}
D^2\mathcal L_{\rm KKT}(z_\star)
P_{\rm phys}.
\end{equation}

If $P_2$ denotes the projector onto the first admissible physical
scalar-topographic harmonic, the constraint-eliminated quadratic operator is
the Schur--Feshbach reduction
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_2
=
P_2
\left[
\widehat{\mathcal H}
-
\widehat{\mathcal H}Q_2
\left(
Q_2\widehat{\mathcal H}Q_2
\right)^{-1}
Q_2\widehat{\mathcal H}
\right]
P_2 ,
\label{eq:bhsm-K2}
\end{equation}
whenever the eliminated block is invertible on the chosen physical domain.
Here $Q_2=1-P_2$ on the already gauge-reduced tangent space.

\subsection{Cosmological topographic-mode theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem 1 (action-owned $n=2$ envelope transport).}
Assume that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the cosmological parent envelope is the closed spatial manifold
      $\mathcal E_{\rm cos}\simeq S^3(R_H)$;
\item the scalar/topographic perturbation belongs to the BHSM
      action-owned boundary-mode sector and is reduced to
      $T_{\rm phys}$ before physical propagation is assigned;
\item the homogeneous $n=0$ component is absorbed into the background and
      the exceptional scalar $n=1$ sector is removed by the regular
      closed-FLRW constraint/gauge quotient;
\item the reduced $n=2$ kinetic form is nondegenerate and positive on the
      retained mode.
\end{enumerate}

Then the first physical scalar-topographic carrier is an action-owned
$n=2$ envelope mode.  It does not require the introduction of an additional
fundamental bulk scalar degree of freedom.  Its canonical momentum, when
nonzero, is induced by the reduced action itself and is therefore conjugate
data of the same geometric mode rather than a separately postulated field.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Let $e_2(x)$ be a normalized element of
$P_2T_{\rm phys}$ and write the retained perturbation locally as
\begin{equation}
\delta\Phi_{\rm topo}(t,x)=q_2(t)e_2(x).
\end{equation}
Substitution into the gauge- and constraint-reduced quadratic action gives,
up to linear source terms and a choice of time parametrization,
\begin{equation}
S^{(2)}_2
=
\frac12
\int dt\,a^3
\left[
\mathcal G_2(t)\,
(D_t q_2)^2
-
\mathcal M_2^2(t)\,q_2^2
+
2\,\mathcal J_2(t)\,q_2
\right].
\label{eq:bhsm-q2-action}
\end{equation}
The coefficients are the $e_2$ matrix elements of the reduced operator
\eqref{eq:bhsm-K2}; explicitly,
$\mathcal G_2$ is the kinetic quadratic form,
$\mathcal M_2^2$ the reduced spatial/restoring form, and
$\mathcal J_2$ the projection of the action-owned source onto $e_2$.
The canonical momentum is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Pi_2
=
a^3\mathcal G_2 D_tq_2 ,
\end{equation}
which is generated by the same reduced action.  Variation of
\eqref{eq:bhsm-q2-action} yields
\begin{equation}
D_t\!\left(a^3\mathcal G_2D_tq_2\right)
+
a^3\mathcal M_2^2 q_2
=
a^3\mathcal J_2 .
\label{eq:bhsm-q2-eom}
\end{equation}
Thus the dynamical phase-space pair $(q_2,\Pi_2)$ is the reduced description
of one action-owned envelope mode.  No second ontological scalar is created
by introducing an effective coordinate for that mode. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Relation to the exact closed-Horndeski kernel}

The closed-Horndeski calculation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:closed-horndeski-n2} is used here as a late-time effective
response representation of the same reduced mode, not as the microscopic
definition of the BHSM topographic ontology.  In the Bellini--Sawicki
convention used in that section, the exact physical-$n=2$ kinetic coefficient
is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{S,2}
=
M_{\rm Pl}^2
\frac{
5D_{\rm kin}
}{
D_{\rm kin}
+
10\left(1-\alpha_B^{\rm BS}/2\right)^2
}.
\label{eq:bhsm-horndeski-match}
\end{equation}
For $D_{\rm kin}>0$ and nonsingular braiding denominator this coefficient is
positive.  Equation~\eqref{eq:bhsm-horndeski-match} therefore supplies an
effective kinetic matching condition for assumption 4 of Theorem~1 on the
reference branch.

The Stueckelberg variable $\pi$ used in the EFT equations is accordingly
treated as a coordinate representative of the retained response of $q_2$.
It is not counted as an additional BHSM mode.  A change of EFT normalization
amounts to an invertible local redefinition of the same reduced coordinate.

\subsection{Exact observer dipole inside the physical $n=2$ sector}

A degree-two harmonic on $S^3$ may be written
\begin{equation}
T_2=A_{AB}X^AX^B,\qquad A_A{}^A=0 .
\end{equation}
Relative to an observer shell,
\begin{equation}
T_2
=
A_{44}\left(c^2-\frac{s^2}{3}\right)
+
2cs\,\mathbf a\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf n}
+
s^2 S_{ij}\hat n^i\hat n^j .
\end{equation}
The pure $A_{4i}$ subspace is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
T_{2,\rm dip}
=
A\sin(2\psi)
\left(
\hat{\mathbf n}\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf p}
\right)
},
\label{eq:bhsm-n2-exact-dipole}
\end{equation}
so the observed dipolar angular dependence is embedded exactly in the first
physical scalar-topographic harmonic.

\subsection{Role of the curved EFT Bianchi audit}

The curvature-aware EFT system of the following sections remains a valuable
cross-check of a chosen four-dimensional response representation.  It is not
used as the ontological definition of the BHSM mode and it is not allowed to
introduce an additional independent scalar degree of freedom.

At the present stage, the literal curvature-aware $00$, $0i$, spatial-trace,
and scalar equations and the reference background have been independently
transcription-audited.  The fully integrated curved-EFT differential-
algebraic representation nevertheless retains a nonzero constraint-
preservation defect, while the consistently flattened system closes to
numerical precision.  We therefore classify that calculation as an
\emph{EFT compatibility audit} rather than as the defining transport theorem.

The defining evolution statement for the bridge is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bhsm-q2-eom}: propagation is generated by the action-reduced
physical $n=2$ operator.  A future exact identification of the curved EFT
transfer map with $\mathcal K_2$ must reproduce this reduced phase space
without duplicating degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Claim boundary}

Theorem~1 establishes the mode ownership and the form of its reduced
transport law conditional on the BHSM action-owned scalar/topographic sector,
the physical quotient, and positivity of the retained kinetic form.  It does
not by itself determine an absolute normalization for
$\mathcal M_2^2$, $\mathcal J_2$, or the observable transfer coefficient.
Those quantities are supplied, where stated, by the frozen reference branch
or by explicit observational calibration and are labelled accordingly.

Consequently, the exact $n=2$ dipole geometry, the physical-harmonic
selection, and the positive effective kinetic matching are structural results.
Reference growth curves and other quantities that require a particular EFT
transfer realization remain reference-branch predictions rather than
framework-wide theorems.
