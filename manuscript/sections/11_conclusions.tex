
\section{Conclusions}

We have constructed a reduced-response bridge between hyperspherical scalar-topographic cosmology and the Berger--Hopf response architecture. The model retains a covariant cubic-Galileon background and interprets the higher-spatial-gradient topographic stiffness as the low-energy effect of eliminating a coupled physical complement.

On \(S^3(R_H)\), the exact scalar spectrum
\[
p_n=\frac{n(n+2)}{a^2R_H^2}
\]
allows a simple harmonic filter. For
\[
\frac18<\ell^2<\frac13,
\]
the same reduced spatial response that softens \(n=1\) stiffens every \(n\ge2\) level. This produces a hierarchy in compact-space susceptibility without forcing a large modification of ordinary structure-growth modes. The \(n=2\) bound is a compact-harmonic statement and is not, without projection, a direct sky-quadrupole prediction.

The exact \(n=1\) scalar field has a monopole-plus-dipole observer-sky form. Its metric interpretation is more restrictive: under the standard closed-universe harmonic convention our \(n=1\) level maps to the exceptional \(N=2\) sector of gauge-invariant closed-FLRW perturbation theory \citep{KieferVardanyan2022}. Consequently, metric and luminosity-distance predictions from this mode remain conditional until the exceptional sector is derived consistently from the constrained scalar--metric system.

The carried-forward low-redshift Pantheon+SH0ES calibration is used only to normalize the proposed observed response. The pre-existing curvature forecast
\[
\Omega_k=-0.018\pm0.004
\]
is kept separate and frozen as a falsifiable forecast rather than presented as a current best fit. An exploratory reference branch R1 gives a late-time braiding component that is negligible at high redshift and, under a background-only high-\(n\) growth approximation, predicts a few-percent shift of \(f\sigma_8\) relative to matched curved \(\Lambda\)CDM over the main survey range.

The prospective signature is therefore a pattern:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{enhanced first nonconstant }S^3\text{ response}\\
\oplus\ \text{suppressed higher-}n\text{ susceptibility}\\
\oplus\ \text{near-GR high-}k\text{ growth}
\end{gathered}
}
\]
The accompanying preregistration protocol is designed so that curvature, harmonic hierarchy, fixed-axis anisotropy, growth, lensing, and the exceptional-mode gauge test can falsify the bridge without post-comparison retuning.
