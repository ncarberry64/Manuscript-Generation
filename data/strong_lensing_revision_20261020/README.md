# Independent HE 0435-1223 LCO observations

These are third-party public observations, retained as original bytes for a new retrospective confirmatory test. The prior experiment consumed the full 2020 COSMOGRAIL release; those data are now development data. The new exposure set comes from independent Las Cumbres Observatory 1 m CTIO telescopes and extends through 17 January 2024.

The source is C. Sorgenfrei, R. W. Schmidt and J. Wambsganss (2025), *Detection of colour variations from gravitational microlensing observations in the quadruple quasar HE0435-1223: Implications for the accretion disc*, A&A **703**, A250, [doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202555933](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202555933). The original catalogue is [CDS J/A+A/703/A250](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/703/A250); its methods are available in the [authors' primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09341v1).

`unused_observation_metadata.json` records original URLs, byte counts, SHA-256 hashes, timestamp vectors, column layout, observational provenance and limitations. `r_confirmatory_split.json` records the parent-selected time cut and test channels. The acquisition utility parses only the first ten bytes of each data row, its timestamp, and never parses or prints photometric targets.

The R-band split is fixed at **MJD 58484**, 1 January 2019, beyond all previously consumed COSMOGRAIL observations even with their half-day origin ambiguity. The 25 earlier R epochs in `calibration/he0435r_pre2019.dat` may be used for model and instrument calibration. The 54 later R epochs in `sealed/he0435r_2019onward.dat` contain 216 magnitudes: the primary test declares image C to be an observed driver and predicts 162 held-out image A/B/D magnitudes; the secondary test predicts all 216. The cut and C-driver choice were made before numerical LCO target inspection. Both files preserve complete original row bytes, and their concatenation exactly reproduces the original 79-row R release. The original V release remains sealed and unscored.

The independent CTIO exposures are not a re-reduction of the old Euler/Maidanak/Mercator exposures. The post-2018 subset also comprises later source epochs. The final published LCO photometry is nevertheless a retrospective common reduction: its difference-imaging reference has substantial 2022 contribution, so reference-flux/reduction errors can correlate calibration and test epochs. This is not an independently blinded image reduction. During source identification, the primary paper's abstract/method text disclosed qualitative published variability findings and aggregate modeling results. No catalogue photometric targets, plots, our predictions or confirmatory scores were inspected by the metadata acquirer. Publication-level awareness must not be described as complete scientific blinding.

The columns are MJD, magnitude A, uncertainty A, magnitude B, uncertainty B, magnitude C, uncertainty C, magnitude D, uncertainty D, and band. Quoted uncertainties are marginal 1-sigma magnitude errors; no full common-image or common-reference covariance is supplied. The magnitudes have an apparent R zero point estimated from Gaia stars, whereas the COSMOGRAIL release has arbitrary relative zeros. Per-image instrument/reduction offsets are required. CDS labels the epoch MJD without specifying its time scale or a heliocentric correction. A cross-instrument time-map nuisance or declared sensitivity is required; sub-day equivalence to COSMOGRAIL MHJD is not silently assumed.
